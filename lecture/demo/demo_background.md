## Notes on Procedure

The experiment you participated in online was very similar to the one that we designed together in the [session on 19/10/2026](https://jackedtaylor.github.io/expra-wise26/general/schedule.html#part-1-introduction-and-the-demo-experiment).

**Software**

One large difference from the study that we built together is that, rather than Psychopy, the experiment was built using [JSPsych](https://www.jspsych.org/latest/) (de Leeuw et al., [2023](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.05351)), and run via [Pavlovia](https://pavlovia.org/#about).

**Instructions**

Participants were instructed to...

> Press M for a word and X for a pseudoword. Respond as quickly and accurately as you can.

**Visual Angles**

Display size and the participant's distance were estimated using the "virtual chinrest" method (Li et al., [2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-57204-1)). This allowed us to present stimuli at specific visual angles of size and eccentricity.

* The fixation was a simple "cross" (+ symbol), with a height of 0.5 degrees of visual angle.

* Words and pseudowords had a maximum height of 1.25 degrees of visual angle.

* Words and pseudowords had an eccentricity of 2.5 degrees of visual angle, from the fixation to the centre of the word. 

**Timing**

In each trial:

1. The fixation was presented centrally for a random duration between 400 and 700 ms.

2. The stimulus was presented immediately afterwards, in either the left or right visual field, for a duration of 250 ms.

3. If participants did not respond by 2500 ms after stimulus presentation, then a reminder of the task was presented in the centre of the screen. 

**Practice Trials**

There were 10 practice trials (5 words, 5 pseudowords). After each practice trial, participants were presented with feedback: "Correct!" in green, or "Incorrect!" in red.

**Breaks**

There were self-paced breaks in the main experiment every 50 trials. Here, participants were asked to take a short break, and presented with a summary of their performance so far (mean accuracy and median response time).
