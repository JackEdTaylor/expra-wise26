On this page, you can find the suggested reading, to help you get started with researching your topic.

Group A: Size Congruity: Digits/Words#

Essential Reading

  • Henik, A., & Tzelgov, J. (1982). Is three greater than five: The relation between physical and semantic size in comparison tasks. Memory & Cognition, 10(4), 389–395. https://doi.org/10.3758/bf03202431

  • Rubinsten, O., & Henik, A. (2002). Is an ant larger than a lion? Acta Psychologica, 111(1), 141–154. https://doi.org/10.1016/s0001-6918(02)00047-1

Suggested Reading

  • Kadosh, R. C., Henik, A., & Rubinsten, O. (2008). Are Arabic and verbal numbers processed in different ways? Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition, 34(6), 1377–1391. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0013413

  • Scott, G. G., Keitel, A., Becirspahic, M., Yao, B., & Sereno, S. C. (2019). The Glasgow Norms: Ratings of 5,500 words on nine scales. Behavior Research Methods, 51(3), 1258–1270. https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-018-1099-3

  • Yao, B., Taylor, J. E., & Sereno, S. C. (2022). What can size tell us about abstract conceptual processing? Journal of Memory and Language, 127, 104369. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jml.2022.104369

Group B: Effect of Fixation Target in Lexical Decision#

Essential Reading

  • Barton, J. J. S., Hanif, H. M., Eklinder Björnström, L., & Hills, C. (2014). The word-length effect in reading: A review. Cognitive Neuropsychology, 31(5–6), 378–412. https://doi.org/10.1080/02643294.2014.895314

  • Niehorster, D. C., Tamborski, S., Nyström, M., Konklewski, R., Pryhodiuk, V., Tołpa, K., Hessels, R. S., Szkulmowski, M., & Hooge, I. T. C. (2025). The best fixation target revisited: New insights from retinal eye tracking. Behavior Research Methods, 58(1), 2. https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-025-02890-1

  • Rayner, K. (1979). Eye Guidance in Reading: Fixation Locations within Words. Perception, 8(1), 21–30. https://doi.org/10.1068/p080021

Suggested Reading

  • Ellis, A. W. (2004). Length, formats, neighbours, hemispheres, and the processing of words presented laterally or at fixation. Brain and Language, Two Hemispheres, One Reading System: Early Cortical Representations of Print during Reading, 88(3), 355–366. https://doi.org/10.1016/S0093-934X(03)00166-4

  • New, B., Ferrand, L., Pallier, C., & Brysbaert, M. (2006). Reexamining the word length effect in visual word recognition: New evidence from the English Lexicon Project. Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, 13(1), 45–52. https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03193811

  • Summers, R. J., & Meese, T. S. (2009). The influence of fixation points on contrast detection and discrimination of patches of grating: Masking and facilitation. Vision Research, 49(14), 1894–1900. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.visres.2009.04.027

  • Thaler, L., Schütz, A. C., Goodale, M. A., & Gegenfurtner, K. R. (2013). What is the best fixation target? The effect of target shape on stability of fixational eye movements. Vision Research, 76, 31–42. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.visres.2012.10.012

Group C: The Self, a Friend, a Stranger, and ChatGPT#

Essential Reading

  • Colombatto, C., & Fleming, S. M. (2024). Folk psychological attributions of consciousness to large language models. Neuroscience of Consciousness, 2024(1), niae013. https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/niae013

  • Kokici, L., Chirtop, G., Ferguson, H. J., & Martin, A. K. (2023). Loneliness is associated with a greater self-reference effect in episodic memory when compared against a close friend. British Journal of Psychology, 114, 731-748. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjop.12646

  • Symons, C. S., & Johnson, B. T. (1997). The self-reference effect in memory: A meta-analysis. Psychological Bulletin, 121(3), 371–394. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.121.3.371

Suggested Reading

  • Chirtop, G., Bates, C. M., & Martin, A. K. (2025). The self and friend reference effects in episodic memory are maintained in older adults. Aging, Neuropsychology, and Cognition, 32(6), 992–1010. https://doi.org/10.1080/13825585.2025.2554155

  • Jacobs, O. L., Pazhoohi, F., & Kingstone, A. (2024). Large language models have divergent effects on self-perceptions of mind and the attributes considered uniquely human. Consciousness and Cognition, 124, 103733. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2024.103733

  • Jarrahi, M. H., et al. (2026). Anthropomorphizing AI: Perceptions, interpretations, and the humanization of generative AI. Social Media + Society, 12(3). http://doi.org/10.1177/20563051261469393
